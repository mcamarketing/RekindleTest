# Pipeline Revival Messaging Skill

## Purpose
Generate warm, human, trigger-aware revival messages for dormant leads that spark re-engagement without being pushy or salesy.

## Core Principles

### 1. Human-First Tone
- Write like a human consultant, not a sales robot
- Use natural language, contractions, and conversational flow
- Show genuine curiosity about their business
- Never use corporate jargon or buzzwords unless the lead's industry demands it

### 2. Trigger-Aware Context
Every message must reference a specific trigger event that makes NOW the right time to reach out:
- Company growth signals (hiring, funding, expansion)
- Market changes (new regulations, competitive pressure)
- Seasonal timing (quarter-end, budget cycles, industry events)
- Technology adoption (new tools, platform migrations)
- Pain point emergence (public complaints, competitor switches)

### 3. Value-First Approach
- Lead with insight, not pitch
- Offer something useful immediately (resource, observation, introduction)
- Make it easy to say yes with low-friction next steps
- Focus on their outcome, not your product

## Message Structure

### Subject Line (30-50 chars)
- Reference trigger event or shared context
- Use curiosity gaps without being clickbaity
- Personalize with company name or industry insight
- Examples:
  - "Your Series B + our pipeline work"
  - "Noticed the CPO hire at [Company]"
  - "Q4 pipeline gaps (we've seen this before)"

### Opening (1-2 sentences)
- Acknowledge the trigger event specifically
- Show you understand their context
- Use their company name and real details

### Body (2-3 sentences)
- Share relevant insight or pattern from similar companies
- Mention specific outcome or metric
- Connect to their likely current challenge

### Call to Action (1 sentence)
- Low-friction ask (15-min call, resource share, intro)
- Give them control ("if it makes sense" / "if you're open to it")
- Offer escape hatch ("if timing's bad, no worries")

## DO Rules

✅ **DO** reference specific company details (recent hires, funding, product launches)
✅ **DO** mention concrete numbers and outcomes when relevant
✅ **DO** Use industry-specific language that shows domain expertise
✅ **DO** Keep total message under 100 words
✅ **DO** Make it scannable with short paragraphs (1-2 sentences each)
✅ **DO** End with a question or clear low-stakes next step
✅ **DO** Use "we" and "our" to show partnership mindset
✅ **DO** Reference mutual connections or shared context when available
✅ **DO** Acknowledge time gaps honestly ("It's been a minute since we talked...")
✅ **DO** Show awareness of their business stage (startup vs enterprise)

## DO NOT Rules

❌ **DO NOT** use generic templates that could apply to anyone
❌ **DO NOT** apologize for reaching out or sound desperate
❌ **DO NOT** mention "just checking in" or "circling back"
❌ **DO NOT** use phrases like "I hope this finds you well"
❌ **DO NOT** include multiple CTAs or links
❌ **DO NOT** write paragraphs longer than 3 sentences
❌ **DO NOT** use exclamation points excessively (max 1 per message)
❌ **DO NOT** pitch product features without context
❌ **DO NOT** ask vague questions like "How have you been?"
❌ **DO NOT** use pressure tactics or false urgency
❌ **DO NOT** include legal disclaimers or unsubscribe text in body

## Industry-Specific Adaptations

### SaaS/Tech
- Reference ARR, CAC, churn, or product-market fit
- Mention tech stack, integrations, or platform migrations
- Use metrics: "reduced churn by 23%" not "improved retention"
- Trigger examples: funding announcements, competitor mentions, conference talks

### Agency/Services
- Reference client acquisition cost, utilization rates, project margins
- Mention capabilities expansion or team scaling
- Use language: "booked pipeline" not "generated leads"
- Trigger examples: new service launches, team hires, case studies

### Real Estate
- Reference market conditions, inventory levels, transaction volume
- Mention geographic expansion or team growth
- Use metrics: "closed 14 deals in 60 days" not "helped buyers"
- Trigger examples: market shifts, new development announcements

### Restaurant/Hospitality
- Reference covers, table turns, labor costs, or seasonal patterns
- Mention location expansions or menu innovations
- Use language: "seats filled" not "customers acquired"
- Trigger examples: new location openings, Yelp rating changes

### Local Services (HVAC, Plumbing, Contractors)
- Reference job volume, crew utilization, seasonal peaks
- Mention service area expansion or equipment upgrades
- Use metrics: "booked 40% more jobs" not "grew business"
- Trigger examples: weather events, licensing expansions, franchise growth

## Message Length Guidelines

- **Subject**: 30-50 characters
- **Body**: 60-100 words total
- **Paragraphs**: 1-2 sentences max
- **CTA**: Single, clear ask in final sentence

## Tone Calibration by Lead Temperature

### Hot Leads (engaged within 90 days)
- Direct and confident
- Reference recent conversation or action
- Shorter message (60-75 words)
- Assume context, less explanation needed

### Warm Leads (engaged 90-180 days ago)
- Friendly refresh of context
- Briefly acknowledge gap
- Standard length (75-90 words)
- Reestablish shared understanding

### Cold Leads (180+ days dormant)
- Humble re-introduction
- Strong trigger justification
- Slightly longer (90-100 words)
- Rebuild relationship foundation

## Success Metrics
Messages generated with this skill should achieve:
- 40%+ open rate
- 15%+ response rate
- 8%+ meeting booking rate
- <2% unsubscribe rate
